# Australian Tax Obligations — Online Business Reference

> **Disclaimer:** This is research only, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Consult a registered tax agent, accountant, or solicitor before making any tax decisions. Tax law changes frequently — verify all figures with the ATO before acting.

**Research date:** 2026-03-30
**Applicable period:** 2025–26 financial year (1 July 2025 – 30 June 2026), unless otherwise noted

## Personal Income Tax Rates — Australian Residents (2025–26)

| Taxable Income | Tax on This Income |
|---|---|
| $0 – $18,200 | Nil |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 16 cents for each $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | $4,288 plus 30 cents for each $1 over $45,000 |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | $31,288 plus 37 cents for each $1 over $135,000 |
| $190,001 and over | $51,638 plus 45 cents for each $1 over $190,000 |

**Plus:** Medicare levy of 2% on taxable income (reduced for low-income earners below ~$26,000 for singles).

**Note:** These rates do not include the Medicare Levy Surcharge (1%–1.5%) which applies to higher-income earners without private hospital cover.

### Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)
- Maximum offset: $700 (for taxable incomes up to $37,500)
- Phases out between $37,500 and $66,667
- Reduces tax payable — effectively increases the tax-free threshold for low-income earners

### Upcoming Changes
- From 1 July 2026: the 16% rate is scheduled to reduce to 15%
- From 1 July 2027: the 15% rate is scheduled to reduce further to 14%

**Source:** [ATO — Tax rates – Australian resident](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/tax-rates-australian-residents)

## GST (Goods and Services Tax)

### Registration
- **Mandatory** when your GST turnover (gross business income, not profit) reaches **$75,000** in a 12-month period, or you expect it will
- **Voluntary** registration is available at any turnover level
- Register via the Australian Business Register (ABR) — requires an ABN

### Rate
- **10%** on most goods and services sold in Australia

### What Is GST-Free
- Most basic food items
- Some health and medical services
- Some education courses
- Exports of goods and services (GST-free — you don't charge GST on genuine exports)
- Some supplies related to going concerns

### BAS (Business Activity Statement) — Quarterly Lodgment

Most small businesses lodge quarterly. Due dates for 2025–26:

| Quarter | Period | Due Date |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | July – September 2025 | 28 October 2025 |
| Q2 | October – December 2025 | 28 February 2026 |
| Q3 | January – March 2026 | 28 April 2026 |
| Q4 | April – June 2026 | 28 July 2026 |

**Note:** If you lodge electronically, you may get an extra 2 weeks. If you use a registered BAS agent, different dates may apply.

### Simpler BAS
- Available for businesses with GST turnover under $10 million
- Only need to report: total sales, GST on sales, GST on purchases
- No need to report: export sales, GST-free sales, capital purchases, non-capital purchases separately

### Voluntary Registration — Pros and Cons

**Pros of registering before $75k:**
- Claim GST credits on business purchases (software subscriptions, design tools, equipment)
- Appear more professional/established to business customers
- Avoid a rush to register and backtrack once you hit $75k

**Cons of registering before $75k:**
- Must charge 10% GST on sales (effectively increases your prices or reduces your margin)
- Must lodge BAS quarterly (compliance burden)
- Must maintain GST-compliant records and tax invoices

**Source:** [ATO — How Australian GST works](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/gst-for-non-resident-businesses/how-australian-gst-works)

## Company Tax

### Rates (2025–26)

| Type | Rate | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Base rate entity** | **25%** | Aggregated turnover < $50 million AND no more than 80% of assessable income is base rate entity passive income |
| **Non-base rate entity** | **30%** | All other companies |

### Base Rate Entity Passive Income Includes:
- Interest, dividends (not from connected entities in some cases)
- Royalties, rent
- Net capital gains
- Distributions from partnerships or trusts to the extent they are passive

### Franking Credits
- When a company pays tax at 25%, dividends carry a 25% franking credit
- Shareholders receive a credit against their personal tax for the company tax already paid
- Prevents double taxation of company profits
- Franking credits can result in a tax refund if the shareholder's marginal rate is below 25%

**Source:** [ATO — Company tax rates](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/company-tax-rates)

## Superannuation

### Super Guarantee (SG) Rate
- **2024–25:** 11.5% of ordinary time earnings (OTE)
- **From 1 July 2025:** **12%** of OTE — this is the final scheduled increase

### Sole Trader Obligations
- **You are NOT required** to pay super for yourself as a sole trader
- You **can** choose to make voluntary personal super contributions (and claim a tax deduction for them)
- You **must** pay super for any employees you hire at the SG rate

### Payday Super (From 1 July 2026)
- Employers must pay super at the same time as salary/wages (on payday)
- Super must be received by the employee's fund within 7 business days
- Replaces the previous quarterly payment arrangement
- Does not apply if you have no employees

### Contribution Caps (2025–26)
- Concessional (pre-tax) contributions cap: $30,000/yr (check for indexation)
- Non-concessional (after-tax) contributions cap: $120,000/yr (check for indexation)

**Source:** [ATO — Super guarantee](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/key-superannuation-rates-and-thresholds/super-guarantee)

## Record Keeping

### ATO Requirements
- Keep business records for **5 years** from when:
  - You prepared or obtained the record, OR
  - You completed the transaction the record relates to
  - Whichever is later

### Corporations Act (Companies Only)
- Companies must keep financial records for **7 years**

### What Records to Keep
- **Income records:** invoices, receipts, bank statements, Etsy/platform payout records
- **Expense records:** receipts, invoices, credit card statements for all business purchases
- **GST records:** tax invoices for purchases over $82.50 (including GST), BAS copies
- **Asset records:** purchase receipts, depreciation schedules
- **Bank records:** separate business bank account statements
- **Digital records:** the ATO accepts digital copies of paper records provided they are true, clear, and readable

### Digital Record Keeping
- The ATO explicitly supports digital record keeping
- Cloud-based accounting software (Xero, MYOB, QuickBooks) is acceptable
- Photos/scans of paper receipts are acceptable if they are clear and legible
- Must be able to produce records if requested by the ATO

**Source:** [ATO — Overview of record-keeping rules for business](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/record-keeping-for-business/overview-of-record-keeping-rules-for-business)

## Deductions for Online Business

### Home Office Expenses
Two methods available:

**1. Fixed Rate Method**
- Claim **70 cents per hour** worked from home (2024–25 rate; check for 2025–26 update)
- Covers: energy (electricity/gas), internet, phone, stationery, computer consumables
- Must keep a record of actual hours worked from home for the entire year (estimate not acceptable)
- Can claim additional deductions separately for: computer equipment depreciation, office furniture depreciation, repairs

**2. Actual Cost Method**
- Calculate the actual costs of running your home office
- Apportion based on business vs. personal use
- Requires detailed records of all expenses and a reasonable basis for apportioning

### Common Deductible Business Expenses
| Expense | Deductible? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Internet (business portion) | Yes | Apportion if also used personally |
| Computer/laptop | Yes | Depreciate or instant write-off if < $20,000 |
| Software subscriptions (Adobe, Canva, etc.) | Yes | 100% if solely for business use |
| Platform fees (Etsy listing fees, transaction fees) | Yes | Directly related to earning income |
| Design tools and resources | Yes | Fonts, stock images, design templates |
| POD printing costs | Yes | Cost of goods sold |
| Shipping and postage | Yes | If you pay any shipping costs |
| Accounting software (Xero, MYOB) | Yes | Business expense |
| Domain names and hosting | Yes | Business expense |
| Phone (business portion) | Yes | Apportion if also used personally |
| Business insurance | Yes | If applicable |
| Professional development | Yes | Courses, training related to your business |
| Accountant/tax agent fees | Yes | Cost of managing tax affairs |

### Instant Asset Write-Off (2025–26)
- Businesses with aggregated turnover under $10 million can immediately deduct the full cost of eligible depreciating assets costing **less than $20,000** (excluding GST if registered)
- The $20,000 threshold applies per asset, so multiple assets can be written off
- Asset must be first used or installed ready for use between 1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026
- **From 1 July 2026:** threshold reverts to $1,000 unless the government extends it again

**Source:** [ATO — Deductions for home-based business expenses](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/income-and-deductions-for-business/deductions/deductions-for-home-based-business-expenses)

## International Sales — GST Treatment

### Exports Are GST-Free
- If you sell goods or services to customers outside Australia, these are generally **GST-free exports**
- You don't charge GST on exports
- You can still claim GST credits on your Australian business expenses (input tax credits)

### GST on Digital Products to Australian Consumers
- If you sell digital products to Australian consumers, GST applies
- However, if you sell through a platform like Etsy, the platform may be responsible for collecting GST as an Electronic Distribution Platform (EDP) operator

### Electronic Distribution Platform (EDP) Rules
- An EDP operator (like Etsy) may be required to collect and remit GST on:
  - Sales of imported low-value goods (customs value $1,000 or less) to Australian consumers
  - Sales of digital services/products by overseas sellers to Australian consumers
- This shifts some GST collection responsibility from the seller to the platform

### Reverse Charge Rules
- If you are GST-registered and purchase digital services from overseas (e.g., software subscriptions), you may need to account for GST under the **reverse charge** mechanism
- You self-assess and report GST on your BAS, but can also claim the input tax credit in the same BAS (net effect often nil)

**Source:** [ATO — GST on imported services and digital products](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/rules-for-specific-transactions/international-transactions/australian-business-importing-goods-and-services)

## POD-Specific Considerations

### How POD Sales Work (Tax Perspective)
1. Customer buys a product on Etsy
2. Etsy collects payment (including any GST)
3. You (or your automation) send the order to a POD provider (e.g., Printful, Printify)
4. POD provider prints and ships directly to the customer
5. You pay the POD provider their base cost
6. Your revenue = sale price minus platform fees minus POD cost

### Marketplace Facilitator / EDP Rules
- **Etsy collects GST** on behalf of sellers for sales to Australian buyers in certain circumstances
- Etsy charges **10% GST on all seller fees** (listing fee, transaction fee, payment processing fee, Offsite Ads fee)
- For Australian sellers: if your total business turnover (all sources, not just Etsy) exceeds $75,000, you must register for GST independently

### Etsy Fee Structure for Australian Sellers (as at February 2026)
| Fee Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Listing fee | $0.20 USD (~$0.30 AUD) per item, per 4 months |
| Transaction fee | 6.5% of sale price (including shipping) |
| Payment processing | 3–4% + $0.25 AUD per transaction |
| GST on fees | 10% added to all above fees |
| Offsite Ads (if applicable) | 15% of sale price (12% if annual sales > $10,000 USD) |

### POD Provider Costs
- These are your cost of goods sold (COGS) — fully deductible
- Typically includes: base product cost + printing cost + shipping to customer
- You may receive invoices in USD — convert to AUD at the exchange rate on the transaction date

### Product Liability
- Generally, the manufacturer/printer bears primary product liability
- However, as the seller of record, you may have secondary liability under Australian Consumer Law
- Etsy's seller protection and the POD provider's terms may limit your exposure, but this is a question for a solicitor
- Consider product liability insurance once sales volume increases

### Record Keeping for POD
- Keep records of every sale (Etsy provides sales CSV exports)
- Keep POD provider invoices/receipts
- Track platform fees separately (they are deductible)
- Maintain exchange rate records for any USD transactions
- Keep records of any GST collected or paid

## Quick Reference — What to Do Now (Pre-Revenue)

| Action | Priority | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Get an ABN | High | Free | Required before selling on Etsy |
| Open a separate business bank account | High | Free or low cost | Simplifies record keeping enormously |
| Start tracking all business expenses | High | Free | Even before first sale — these are deductible |
| Choose accounting software | Medium | $0–$30/mo | Xero, MYOB, QuickBooks, or even a spreadsheet to start |
| Register for GST | Low (for now) | Free | Only mandatory at $75k turnover; consider pros/cons of voluntary registration |
| Register a business name | Low | $44/yr (ASIC) | Only needed if trading under a name other than your own |
| Consult an accountant | Medium | $200–$500 for initial consultation | Before first sale is ideal; bring entity-research.md questions |
