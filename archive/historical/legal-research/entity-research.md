## Sole Trader

### How It Works
- Register for an ABN (free via abr.gov.au) — can be done in minutes online
- Business income is your personal income — reported on your individual tax return
- No separation between you and the business

### Tax Treatment
- Taxed at personal marginal rates (see tax-obligations.md for current brackets)
- All business income and expenses reported in the business schedule of your individual tax return
- Eligible for the small business income tax offset (up to $1,000 for individuals with business income from a small business entity with aggregated turnover less than $5 million)
- $20,000 instant asset write-off available (for assets first used or installed ready for use before 30 June 2026; threshold reverts to $1,000 from 1 July 2026 unless extended)
- Eligible for small business CGT concessions

### Advantages
- Zero setup cost (ABN is free)
- Simplest structure — minimal compliance burden
- Full control — no director duties, no ASIC fees, no annual review
- Tax losses can offset other personal income
- Easy to transition to company/trust later

### Disadvantages
- Unlimited personal liability — personal assets (home, savings, car) are at risk if the business is sued or incurs debts
- No income splitting — all profit taxed at your marginal rate
- Higher tax rate once income exceeds ~$45,000 (30% marginal rate kicks in)
- Less credible appearance to some business partners/suppliers
- If you die or become incapacitated, the business has no legal continuity

### Registrations Required
- ABN (free)
- Business name registration with ASIC if trading under a name other than your own ($44 for 1 year, $102 for 3 years — check current ASIC fees)
- GST registration (mandatory if turnover reaches $75,000; voluntary before that)
- TFN (you likely already have one)

## Company (Pty Ltd)

### How It Works
- Register with ASIC — creates a separate legal entity
- You become a director (and typically sole shareholder for a solo operator)
- The company earns income, pays tax, and can pay you a salary or dividends

### Current ASIC Fees (2025–26 Financial Year)
- **Company registration:** $611
- **Annual review fee:** $329/yr (proprietary company)
- **Late fee:** additional penalty if annual review not completed on time
- Fees are indexed annually to CPI on 1 July

### Tax Treatment
- **Base rate entity:** 25% company tax rate — applies if aggregated turnover is less than $50 million AND no more than 80% of assessable income is base rate entity passive income
- **Non-base rate entity:** 30% company tax rate
- Franking credits attach to dividends paid to shareholders (reduces double taxation)
- Company must lodge its own tax return (separate from your personal return)
- Must register for PAYG withholding if paying yourself a salary

### Advantages
- Limited liability — personal assets are generally protected
- Flat 25% tax rate (base rate entity) is lower than top personal marginal rates
- Franking credits make dividends tax-effective
- Perpetual existence — business continues regardless of owner
- More credible structure for business-to-business dealings

### Disadvantages
- Setup cost ($611 + professional fees)
- Ongoing ASIC annual review ($329/yr)
- Must maintain proper company records, minutes, registers
- Director duties under the Corporations Act 2001 (personal liability for breaches)
- Cannot distribute losses to shareholders
- Less flexible than a trust for income distribution
- Redundant compliance cost if business is pre-revenue or very low revenue

### Key Compliance Obligations
- ASIC annual review (within 2 months of review date)
- Company tax return (annually)
- Maintain registered office and principal place of business
- Keep financial records for 7 years (Corporations Act requirement — note: longer than ATO's 5-year rule)
- Notify ASIC of changes to company details within 28 days

## Discretionary (Family) Trust

### How It Works
- A solicitor drafts a trust deed that establishes the trust
- A trustee (person or company) manages the trust's assets on behalf of beneficiaries
- The trustee has discretion to distribute income to any beneficiary each year
- The trust itself is not a separate legal entity — it's a relationship

### Setup Costs
- Trust deed: $800–$2,000+ (must be drafted by a solicitor; DIY deeds risk being invalid)
- ABN registration: free
- Stamp duty on trust deed: varies by state/territory (some states charge nil or nominal; check your state)

### Tax Treatment
- Trust itself does not pay tax (unless income is not distributed)
- Trustee distributes income to beneficiaries each year — beneficiaries pay tax at their marginal rates
- **Critical rule:** undistributed income is taxed to the trustee at the highest marginal rate (45% + 2% Medicare levy = 47%)
- Trust must distribute ALL net income by 30 June each year
- 50% CGT discount available (held >12 months), plus small business CGT concessions
- Cannot carry forward losses at the trust level to offset against beneficiaries' other income (losses are trapped in the trust)

### Advantages
- Highly flexible income distribution — can direct income to lower-taxed beneficiaries (family members)
- Good asset protection — assets are owned by the trust, not the individual
- 50% CGT discount for assets held more than 12 months
- Effective for multiple ventures — one trust can run several businesses
- Estate planning benefits — assets pass outside of the will

### Disadvantages
- Higher setup cost ($800–$2,000+ for solicitor-drafted deed)
- Must distribute all income annually or face top marginal rate
- Losses are trapped in the trust — cannot offset personal income
- More complex tax return (trust tax return + individual returns for beneficiaries)
- Trust deed is difficult/expensive to amend once established
- ATO scrutiny — the ATO closely monitors trust distributions for compliance with Section 100A (reimbursement agreements)

### ATO Scrutiny — Section 100A
- The ATO has increased focus on trust distribution arrangements
- Distributions to adult children or other low-income beneficiaries where the money is ultimately used by/returned to the primary income earner may be challenged
- Genuine distributions to beneficiaries who actually receive and use the funds are acceptable

## Hybrid Structure: Trust + Company

### How It Works
- A discretionary trust is established with a Pty Ltd company as the trustee (corporate trustee)
- The trust operates the business
- The trust can distribute income to the corporate trustee (acting in its own capacity, not as trustee) — this is the "bucket company" strategy
- The bucket company pays tax at 25% on income distributed to it, capping the tax rate

### Setup Costs
- Trust deed: $800–$2,000+
- Company registration (ASIC): $611
- Professional fees for structuring: $2,000–$5,000+
- Total first-year cost: $3,000–$7,000+ including accountant fees

### Tax Treatment — Bucket Company Strategy
- Trust earns business income
- Trustee distributes income to beneficiaries at their marginal rates (optimal for low-income beneficiaries)
- Excess income distributed to the bucket company at 25% flat rate (capping the tax)
- The company can then pay dividends to shareholders later (with franking credits)
- Effectively caps maximum tax rate at 25% for retained earnings (vs. 45%+ personal rate)

### Advantages
- Maximum flexibility — combines trust distribution flexibility with company tax cap
- Best asset protection — corporate trustee limits trustee's personal liability
- Bucket company caps tax at 25% for income not distributed to individuals
- Suitable for a portfolio of ventures under one structure
- Best for established businesses with significant income

### Disadvantages
- Most expensive to set up and maintain
- Two entities to maintain (trust + company) — two tax returns, ASIC annual review
- Ongoing professional fees: $3,000–$5,000+/yr for accounting
- Complexity may be unwarranted for pre-revenue or low-revenue businesses
- ATO scrutiny of bucket company arrangements
- Division 7A rules — loans between the company and trust/beneficiaries must comply strictly or face deemed dividend treatment

### When It Makes Sense
- Business income consistently exceeds the 30%+ marginal tax brackets
- Multiple family members who could be genuine beneficiaries
- Asset protection is a priority (e.g., higher-risk product categories)
- Multiple ventures generating substantial combined revenue

## For Our Specific Situation

### Current Profile
- Solo operator in Australia
- Pre-revenue — no business income yet
- Multiple planned online ventures (POD, digital products)
- Low product liability risk (print-on-demand fulfillment handled by third parties)
- Marketplace-based sales (Etsy, potentially Redbubble) — platform handles some compliance

### Considerations

**Starting as a sole trader makes sense when:**
- You are pre-revenue or low-revenue
- You want to validate the business model before committing to structure costs
- Annual compliance costs ($329+ ASIC fee, $1,500+ accountant fees) would consume revenue
- You need simplicity — focus on building the business, not maintaining a structure
- You can transition to a company or trust later when income justifies it

**Transitioning to a company or trust makes sense when:**
- Consistent annual profit exceeds ~$45,000–$50,000 (where marginal tax rate exceeds 25%)
- You want asset protection (e.g., product liability concerns)
- You have family members who could genuinely receive trust distributions
- The cost of maintaining the structure ($2,000–$5,000+/yr) is a small fraction of revenue
- You plan to reinvest profits in the business (retained earnings taxed at 25% in company)

**Common transition path:**
1. Start as sole trader (validate, build, earn first revenue)
2. Once profitable and growing, transition to discretionary trust with corporate trustee
3. Use bucket company strategy to cap tax at 25% on retained earnings

> **Important:** Changing structure later has CGT and other tax implications. Discuss timing with an accountant BEFORE the transition, not after.

## Questions to Ask an Accountant or Solicitor

### Entity Structure
1. Given my situation (solo, pre-revenue, multiple online ventures), should I start as a sole trader and transition later, or set up a trust/company from the start?
2. What are the CGT implications of transitioning from sole trader to a trust or company down the track?
3. If I set up a discretionary trust, what should the trust deed include to accommodate multiple online business ventures?
4. Is a corporate trustee worth the extra cost at this stage, or can I be an individual trustee initially?

### Tax
5. At what income level does a company or trust structure start saving me tax compared to sole trader?
6. How do franking credits work in practice for a sole shareholder/director?
7. What is the most tax-effective way to pay myself from a company — salary, dividends, or a combination?
8. How does the bucket company strategy work in practice, and what are the Division 7A risks?

### Compliance
9. What are the ongoing annual costs I should budget for each structure type?
10. What record-keeping systems do you recommend for a solo online business?
11. When should I register for GST voluntarily vs. waiting until the $75k threshold?
12. Are there any state/territory-specific considerations (stamp duty on trust deeds, etc.)?

### Platform-Specific
13. How does Etsy's GST collection as an electronic distribution platform (EDP) affect my BAS reporting?
14. For POD where a third-party printer fulfills the order, who is responsible for product liability — me, the printer, or the platform?
15. How should I treat international sales (to US, UK, EU customers) for GST purposes?

## Key Thresholds to Monitor

| Threshold | Amount | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| **GST registration** | $75,000 turnover | Must register for GST and lodge BAS |
| **Base rate entity** | < $50M aggregated turnover + < 80% passive income | Qualifies for 25% company tax rate (vs. 30%) |
| **Tax-free threshold** | $18,200 | No tax on first $18,200 of taxable income (individuals) |
| **16% bracket ends** | $45,000 | Marginal rate increases from 16% to 30% |
| **30% bracket ends** | $135,000 | Marginal rate increases from 30% to 37% |
| **37% bracket ends** | $190,000 | Marginal rate increases from 37% to 45% |
| **Medicare levy** | All taxable income (above threshold) | 2% of taxable income |
| **PAYG instalments** | Varies (ATO notifies you) | Quarterly prepayments of expected tax liability |
| **Instant asset write-off** | < $20,000 per asset | Immediate deduction for assets under threshold (until 30 June 2026) |
| **Small business CGT concessions** | < $2M net assets or < $10M aggregated turnover | Various CGT reliefs on sale of business assets |
| **Superannuation guarantee** | 12% of ordinary time earnings | Must pay super for employees (not mandatory for sole trader paying self, but recommended) |

## Next Steps

1. **Discuss with accountant** — take this research and the questions above to a registered tax agent or accountant for personalised advice
2. **Register ABN** — regardless of structure chosen, an ABN is needed (free via abr.gov.au)
